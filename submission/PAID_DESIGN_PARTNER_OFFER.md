# Driftline paid design-partner offer

Status: local draft only. Do not send, post, publish, invoice, or represent this
as accepted without Mike approving the exact recipient, price, and terms.

## Brutal feasibility

A real paying customer in the next few days is possible and unlikely. The
highest-probability outcome is one qualified PM session plus a scheduled price
conversation. The cleanest customer path is a small fixed-fee pilot that a
founder or fractional product leader can approve without procurement.

Do not discount a free interview into a customer claim. Customer status begins
only after payment or a signed commitment to the paid scope below.

## The offer

**One Decision Under Pressure — $500 fixed-fee design-partner pilot**

For one current B2B SaaS rollout, roadmap, pricing, packaging, platform, or
segment decision due within 30 days, Driftline will deliver:

- a private 45-minute redacted decision session;
- one evidence-bound brief with competing recommendations and visible dissent;
- one named decision owner, affected segment, primary metric, risk guardrail,
  stop threshold, rollback, and review window;
- one saved non-confidential case-return link;
- one 30-minute outcome review after the agreed measurement window; and
- one revision if the participant identifies a material citation or framing
  error during the initial session.

The participant receives the brief and learning receipt for internal use.
Driftline receives candid product feedback and permission to retain only the
commercial facts required for the engagement. Public attribution, quotations,
screenshots, company identity, and decision details are excluded unless the
participant separately approves an exact disclosure in writing.

## Boundaries

- Redacted, non-confidential inputs only.
- No customer records, personal data, regulated data, credentials, or secrets.
- No external system writes and no claim that the model made the decision.
- The named human remains the decision authority.
- PM-provided evidence and outcome aggregates remain labeled unverified.
- This is a decision-quality pilot, not a promise of revenue, retention, or ROI.
- If the decision is not real, not due within 30 days, or has no measurable
  downside, decline or route it to a free research interview instead.

## Qualification before price

Ask these four questions before presenting the paid offer:

1. What decision must be made, by whom, and by what date?
2. What are the two most plausible choices and the downside of being wrong?
3. Can the decision be represented using safely redacted evidence?
4. What observable primary metric and risk guardrail could reality update?

If all four answers are concrete, show the product on that decision. Discuss
price only after the participant agrees the resulting brief is useful enough
to continue into the outcome review.

## Close test

> If you want to use this on the live decision and return for the measured
> outcome, the design-partner pilot is $500 fixed fee for this one decision,
> including the follow-up and one revision. Nothing is published or attributed.
> Is that worth doing for this decision?

A yes followed by payment or a signed paid commitment is a customer. “Send me
more information,” praise, an introduction, a free follow-up, or a price
question without commitment is not.

## Codex prospecting boundary

Codex can repeatedly research public prospects, verify current roles and
contact routes, score fit, and draft individualized messages. It should not
mass-message, submit contact forms, RSVP, register, email, or claim a customer.
Mike approves every exact recipient and final message; the recipient supplies
the decision and chooses whether to pay.

An ongoing research task should produce at most five fresh, high-confidence
prospects per run and include:

- current role and public source;
- observed signal of an urgent decision category;
- why the prospect can plausibly authorize a small pilot;
- the safest public contact route;
- a one-paragraph individualized draft; and
- disqualifying evidence or uncertainty.

Stop adding names when the list becomes less qualified. The bottleneck is a
trusted introduction and a live decision, not lead volume.
