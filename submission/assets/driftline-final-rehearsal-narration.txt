A roadmap decision becomes dangerous when the evidence changes but the commitment does not. Driftline catches that drift, compares the safest responses, and keeps the measured outcome attached to the original call.

Driftline makes four alternatives concrete before anyone commits. Shipping protects momentum but risks enterprise conversion. Rolling back loses small-team gains. Deferring buys information but misses the launch window. A segmented rollout preserves upside and contains the observed failure mode.

The model recommends; a named human authorizes. Approval freezes a falsifiable experiment and creates one bounded internal allocation in Driftline's own decision state: exact segment, owner, metric, guardrail, stop rule, rollback, and review date. The receipt states external writes: none.

When the enterprise guardrail breaks, Driftline rolls the internal allocation back, writes a learning receipt, and reopens the same decision as generation two.
